ft_transcendence

A real-time multiplayer web arena for Tic-Tac-Toe and Connect Four, built with a five-person team — from a frozen API contract to a fully defended, deployed product.

01

The Problem

An open brief, five people, one deadline

The subject doesn't specify what to build — just a menu of optional feature modules worth points, with a 14-point minimum to pass. Five of us had to agree on a single product, split it into independently ownable pieces, and build in parallel without our code colliding or surprises appearing at integration time.

02

The Build

The shell, the polish, and pulling it together

As PO/PM I owned the parts that turn five people's separate modules into one coherent product: the app shell — layout, navigation, footer — the lobby for choosing an opponent or watching a live match, the settings page, the profile and stats views, and the public leaderboard. I also wrote real Privacy Policy and Terms of Service pages with project-specific content, and an automated check that keeps the browser console clear of any JavaScript warning or error across the entire app.

03

The Result

14 points, defended, deployed behind HTTPS

The finished app lets you sign up, play Tic-Tac-Toe or Connect Four live against another logged-in player or an AI opponent, spectate other people's matches, and track your stats on a public leaderboard — all served from a single Docker container behind HTTPS. Before defense, I ran a hardening pass against the real built container: concurrent sessions, a dropped connection mid-game, and a broken URL, so the demo held up under real conditions.
